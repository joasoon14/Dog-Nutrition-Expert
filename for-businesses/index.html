Product development questions

Common questions about dog food product development

Answers to common questions about recipe development, nutritional evaluation, ingredient selection, production methods, and starting a new dog food project.

Can you develop a complete dog food recipe from scratch?

Yes. I can develop a new dog food recipe from the initial product concept through ingredient selection, nutritional calculations, and recipe balancing, taking into account the intended product type, production method, and nutritional goals.

Can you review and improve an existing dog food recipe?

Yes. I can evaluate an existing recipe and identify possible improvements in nutritional balance, ingredient selection, ingredient proportions, or overall formulation, depending on the product goals and production requirements.

Can you evaluate whether a dog food recipe meets FEDIAF nutritional guidelines?

Yes. I can evaluate the nutritional composition of a recipe against relevant FEDIAF nutritional guidelines and identify nutrients that may be below or above the recommended levels. If needed, the recipe can then be adjusted accordingly.

Can you help select ingredients for a dog food product?

Yes. Ingredient selection can be based on nutritional value, functionality, product concept, processing method, availability, and practical production considerations. I can also help compare different ingredient options and their contribution to the final recipe.

Can you adapt a dog food recipe to a specific production method?

Yes. A recipe can be adapted according to the intended production process, such as fresh, raw, air-dried, or freeze-dried products. Processing conditions can affect moisture, nutrient concentrations, ingredient functionality, product structure, and other characteristics, so the production method should be considered during formulation.

What information do you need to start a dog food development project?

To begin, I need an overview of the product you want to develop, the ingredients you would like to use, and the intended production process. If the product is processed, information such as processing temperatures, times, drying conditions, or other relevant production parameters is helpful. It is also useful to know the intended product type, target nutritional profile, any ingredient restrictions, available raw materials, and whether a recipe or product concept already exists.
