Dog nutrition questions

Common questions about feeding your dog

Answers to common questions about feeding amounts, balanced diets, raw and home-cooked food, commercial dog food, food labels, and changing your dog's diet.

How much food should I feed my dog per day?

The right amount of food depends on your dog's weight, age, life stage, activity level, body condition, and the energy density of the food. Feeding guides are useful starting points, but the final amount should be adjusted according to your dog's individual response over time.

How do I know if my dog's diet is nutritionally balanced?

A balanced dog diet should provide appropriate amounts of protein, fat, essential fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals in relation to the dog's energy needs. Even a diet made from high-quality ingredients can have nutritional gaps, so home-prepared diets are best evaluated using nutritional calculations.

Can I make complete dog food at home?

Yes, a nutritionally complete home-cooked diet can be prepared for a dog, but the ingredients and their proportions need to be chosen carefully. A well-formulated recipe should take into account your dog's energy needs, preferred ingredients, feeding style, and individual nutritional requirements.

How do I balance a raw diet for my dog?

A raw diet does not need to provide every nutrient in exactly the same amount in every single meal, but the overall diet should be nutritionally balanced over time. Using a variety of suitable protein sources and ingredients can help provide dietary variety, although the amount of rotation depends on what your dog tolerates and any individual dietary restrictions.

How do I choose a good commercial dog food?

The best dog food is not necessarily the one with the longest ingredient list or the highest meat percentage. Look for a nutritionally appropriate food that your dog digests well, enjoys eating, maintains a healthy body condition on, and that fits both your dog's individual needs and your everyday feeding routine.

What should I look for on a dog food label?

Look for a clear ingredient list that tells you what the food actually contains, ideally with percentages for important ingredients. When comparing dog foods, also consider moisture content, because protein, fat, and other analytical values can look very different in dry and wet foods simply because of differences in water content.

How should I switch my dog to a new food?

A gradual food transition is usually best, but the right transition period varies between dogs and can range from several days to several weeks or longer. The previous diet, the type of new food, digestive tolerance, and the dog's health should all be considered, while appetite, stool quality, digestion, and overall well-being are monitored during the change.

When should I ask a canine nutrition professional for help?

Professional canine nutrition guidance can be useful when you want to evaluate your dog's current diet, start raw or home-cooked feeding, compare commercial foods, calculate feeding amounts, or create an individual feeding plan. For dogs with diagnosed or suspected health conditions, nutrition can be an important part of supporting overall health and treatment.

Health conditions

Nutrition support alongside veterinary care

Nutrition can be an important part of supporting dogs with diagnosed health conditions. However, nutrition consultations do not replace veterinary examination, diagnosis, monitoring, or medical treatment.

When a dog has a diagnosed or suspected health condition, relevant veterinary information may be needed before dietary recommendations can be prepared. Collaboration with the dog's veterinarian may also be appropriate.
